## Fundamental Analysis

# Hospital Stocks Analysis
* In India we have 4 types of hospital sectors:
  * Primary Care
    * It's like a PHC. Which are mostly in remote areas and not listed in Markets.
  * Secondary Care
    * Here ENT, Appendix, Bone fractures, Gynecology. These are also not listed in markets.
  * Tertiary Care
    * These are listed in Markets. Also, they cover both primary, secondary care along with advanced facilities with ICU, Organ Transplant, Brain Surgery, Heart Surgery, Cancer Treatment. Most hospitals come under this which are listed in markets.
  * Quaternary Care
    * These are ultra super specialty hospitals used for rare genetic treatments, robotic surgeries, High target cancer therapies, Research based treatments. These very less in India. May expect in future also these are very highly expensive.

* Key Points:
  * Our primary focus will be on **Tertiary Care** Hospitals.
  * Most of the income for the hospitals in India will be earning from Oncology, Neurology, Cardiology, Orthopedics.
  * Outpatients will be very high as compared to Inpatients. Almost in 75:25 ratio. But the thing is 80% of the income for the hospitals will be from Inpatients and remaining from the Outpatients.
  * Go through Latest Investor Presentation Before Starting Analysis.

* Important Metrics to Be Considered While Analyzing the Hospital Stock:
  * ARPOB (Average Revenue Per Occupied Bed) - Calculated per day. High ARPOB indicates high Income which will reflect in profits.
  * Geographical Location of the Hospital - Metro Cities are preferred.
  * Mature Hospitals Count Should Be High - Patient Inflow Should be High. Don't consider that they have more count of hospitals (newly established).
  * Payer Mix - Patients (National/International), Insurance, Government. If Patients is the higher payer mix, then it would create good revenue and profits. Income from Insurance & Government won't be settled immediately. Also, Government Caps amount to a certain threshold which will affect the income and settlement period will go for year which is not good sign. So, consider High Patient Payer Mix Ones.

* Business Metrics to Be Considered While Analyzing the Hospital Stock:
  * Consider the Important Metrics Such As ARPOB, Geographical Location, Mature Hospitals, Payer Mix.
  * If ARPOB isn't there consider ARPP (Average Revenue Per Patient).
  * ALOS (Average Length of Stay) - Should be less which increases revenue.
  * Don't consider PE For Hospital Stocks.
  * Consider EV/EBIDTA, Operating Profit, Net Debt/EBIDTA.
  * Occupancy - Should Be High. They Will Try to Maintain (60-70). >70 is very good. Also consider due to addition of new hospitals of them the occupancy got reduced if it's the case then check previous quarter or yearly occupancy.
  * Cash Conversion - Free Cash.
  * Expansion Plan - No of Beds Going to add in next few years. Check the expansion rate which is more than >80% which we will consider as aggressive expansion which will lead to exponential growth in revenue, also consider execution commitments if it fails then it will reflect in the revenue. It doesn't also means the expansion rate less stocks are bad and less growth, but they may increase ARPOB. Also consider the geography of new beds. One more thing to be consider is the Nature (Greenfield, Asset Acquisition, Brownfield).
  * Due to CAPEX the stock may go down but when the hospital gets matured its revenue will picks up back. So, don't panic during the down trend due to capex instead treat it as Buy Oppurtunity.
  * Who is promoting? Private Equity or Family? So, consider **private equity** players in promoting the hospital because the execution will be strict and expansion will be aggressive.
  * Hub & Spoke model is one of the successful ones so consider stocks following this.
